02 / PROBLEM & SOLUTION

The gap is not just language.

Students can know English and still get stuck when a course introduces words that belong to a specific discipline. LingoBridge focuses on that missing layer: context.

01
THE PROBLEM

General English does not automatically mean disciplinary fluency.

A student may understand everyday English but still struggle with the vocabulary, abbreviations, and phrases that lecturers assume everyone already knows. The difficulty can be concentrated in technical terms and the way those terms are used inside a course.

02
MINI RESEARCH / EVIDENCE SCAN

The problem shows up in the research.

75% of participants in a 2026 study of EMI students reported lexical/terminological challenges.

In the same small study (n=12), 56% reported difficulty with technical terms that lacked clear Arabic equivalents. The sample is small, but the finding is directly relevant to multilingual students learning through English.

THE SOLUTION

Put the explanation next to the word.

LingoBridge turns confusing course language into a shared glossary: plain-language explanations, in-class examples, course tags, and peer translations. Students search the meaning they need in the context where they encountered it.

Research on international and second-language university students describes a mismatch between general English requirements and the language demands of particular disciplines. Contextual support can also help learners make sense of technical vocabulary.

LINGOBRIDGE / COMMUNITY GUIDELINES
KEEP THE GLOSSARY USEFUL

What belongs here — and what doesn't.

LingoBridge is for course-specific language that helps students understand class. Contributions should be respectful, relevant, and written to help another student learn.

01

Academic and course-related terms

Add jargon, technical vocabulary, abbreviations, or phrases that students genuinely encounter in lectures, readings, assignments, labs, or projects.

02

No hate speech or slurs

Do not submit words that attack or demean people based on identity, background, nationality, religion, gender, disability, or other protected characteristics.

03

No harassment, threats, or insults

Contributions should explain a term, not target another person. Personal attacks, threats, bullying, and abusive messages are not part of the glossary.

04

No sexual or explicit content

Do not add sexually explicit, pornographic, or deliberately graphic material. A legitimate academic term should be explained in an academic way and kept relevant to the course.

05

No spam, advertising, or unrelated content

Don't use the glossary to promote products, services, links, personal accounts, or content that has nothing to do with understanding course terminology.

06

No private or identifying information

Never submit phone numbers, addresses, passwords, student IDs, private messages, or other personal information about yourself or someone else.

07

Don't copy textbook definitions

Explain the idea in your own words. LingoBridge is strongest when students translate complicated course language into a clear explanation a classmate can actually use.

08

Keep it relevant and honest

Don't deliberately submit misleading, unsafe, or made-up explanations. When you're unsure, say so rather than presenting a guess as a fact.
